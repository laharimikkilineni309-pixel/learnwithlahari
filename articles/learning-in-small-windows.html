Learning Tech in Small Windows of Time as a Mom

Learning does not always happen in a quiet two-hour block. Some days the best available window is twenty minutes. That can still be enough to make progress.

Make the goal small and visible

Instead of "learn backend development," choose a task like "build one GET endpoint" or "solve one hash-map problem." A clear finish line makes it easier to restart after interruptions.

Use three study modes

  • 10 minutes: Read notes, review flashcards, or explain a concept aloud.
  • 25 minutes: Complete a focused coding exercise or debug one failing test.
  • 60 minutes: Build a feature, write tests, and commit your changes.

Keep a restart note

At the end of each session, write down what you completed, what is broken, and the exact next step. This reduces the time spent figuring out where you left off.

Define progress kindly but concretely

Measure projects finished, concepts explained, and problems solved—not hours spent at a desk. If a week gets disrupted, resume with the smallest possible task rather than trying to "catch up" all at once.

Protect your privacy

Share lessons you have learned without feeling obligated to post identifiable family details or photographs. Your story can be personal while keeping boundaries.

Career growth and parenting can coexist, but a sustainable plan needs room for real life.
